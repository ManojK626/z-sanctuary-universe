# ZILWA Elder Rights Charter

> **Governance charter only. Not legal advice. Elder rights arrangements require
> legal review before any community knowledge is shared for tourism purposes.**

## Charter Purpose

This charter protects the dignity, consent, and ownership rights of elders and
knowledge holders in the Flic en Flac and broader Mauritius community. Their
traditions, stories, skills, and lived knowledge belong to them and their families.
ZILWA exists to honour those gifts, not to extract them.

## 1. Dignity Protections

- Every elder is treated as a person of full dignity and agency, not a tourism asset
  or cultural exhibit.
- No elder will be placed in a situation that compromises their comfort, privacy, or
  standing in their community.
- Pacing, physical accessibility, and rest must be accommodated without question.
- Elders set the terms of their participation; hosts follow.

## 2. Consent Protections

- All participation by elders in ZILWA experiences is voluntary and freely given.
- Consent must be informed: the elder must understand what will be shared, with whom,
  how it will be used, and what compensation or recognition applies.
- Consent must be given without pressure, incentive manipulation, or social obligation.
- Consent for any specific use (e.g., recording, publishing, branding) is separate from
  consent to participate generally.
- Consent is revocable at any time. Withdrawal triggers immediate cessation of the
  relevant activity and removal of any related materials.

## 3. Ownership Protections

- All traditional knowledge, stories, techniques, recipes, songs, and practices shared
  by elders remain the property of those elders and their families.
- ZILWA claims no intellectual property over any elder-shared content or cultural
  expression.
- Any documentation, recording, or derivative use requires explicit, specific, written
  agreement with the knowledge holder or their designated representative.
- Ownership does not transfer through informal usage, extended participation, or
  implied consent.

## 4. Right to Withdraw

- Any elder may withdraw from any ZILWA activity or partnership at any time.
- Withdrawal may be complete or partial (covering specific activities, topics, or
  time periods).
- Withdrawal carries no penalty, no reduction in community standing, and no financial
  consequence to the withdrawing elder.
- Withdrawn materials, content, stories, or recordings must be removed from all ZILWA
  channels immediately upon withdrawal.

## 5. Family Ownership Reaffirmation

- Family and community ownership of cultural heritage is recognised and respected.
- Where cultural knowledge is held collectively (family, lineage, community), the
  decision to participate involves appropriate family or community authority, not
  solely one individual.
- ZILWA will not facilitate arrangements that bypass family or community governance
  of shared heritage.

## 6. Non-Commercialisation Posture

- Elder knowledge is not a commercial product. It may be shared in hospitality contexts
  only with the elder's explicit, ongoing, and informed consent.
- No ZILWA pricing model, marketing material, or brand identity may be built upon elder
  knowledge without prior written agreement with the knowledge holder.
- Commercial benefit derived from elder-shared content must flow back to the elder or
  their designated family/community beneficiary through a transparent, documented
  arrangement.

## 7. Respectful Engagement Principles

- Researchers, facilitators, or hosts engaging with elders must demonstrate cultural
  sensitivity and respect for local protocols.
- No topic may be pursued that an elder considers private, sacred, or not for
  external sharing.
- A "sacred/private/not-for-tourism" boundary register must be maintained and honoured.
- Elders will not be asked to simplify, abbreviate, or modify their knowledge for
  entertainment purposes.
- Genuine curiosity, patience, and reciprocity define the engagement posture.

## Charter Status

This charter is a governance starting point. Elder rights in cultural tourism contexts
involve complex legal, ethical, and community dimensions. Independent legal counsel and
community consultation are required before any elder-knowledge participation programme
is formalised.
