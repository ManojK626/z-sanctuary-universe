# ZILWA Carrying Capacity Framework

> Conceptual framework only. Operational capacity thresholds require specialist
> environmental, community, and hospitality review before definition.

## Framework Purpose

"How much is too much?" is not a rhetorical question. It is the central discipline
question of sustainable community tourism. This framework identifies the five dimensions
of capacity that ZILWA must monitor and respect — before launching, not after damage
appears.

## Core Principle

Growth is only good if it can be absorbed. Every ZILWA dimension has a threshold
beyond which the benefit reverses and harm begins. The role of governance is to find
that threshold, set a safe operating range beneath it, and enforce the right to slow
down or stop.

## Capacity Dimensions

### 1. Community Capacity

Describes the ability of Flic en Flac's residents, households, and civic life to absorb
tourism activity without degrading quality of life, social cohesion, or community identity.

Governing question: At what point do residents feel they are living inside a tourism
product rather than in their own neighbourhood?

Overload signals:

- Resident complaints about noise, congestion, or loss of privacy.
- Rising tension between participating and non-participating households.
- Reduction in local use of shared spaces (beaches, village routes, public amenities).
- Reports of community identity being distorted by tourism framing.

Capacity discipline: No ZILWA expansion proceeds unless community capacity indicators
are stable or improving.

### 2. Host-Family Capacity

Describes the sustainable limit of hosting engagement for individual households: the
number of visits, guests, and experience types a family can absorb without burnout,
privacy erosion, or wellbeing degradation.

Governing question: When does welcoming strangers become a burden the household is
absorbing for others' benefit?

Overload signals:

- Hosts reporting fatigue, stress, or reluctance.
- Declining consistency or quality of experiences.
- Host attrition or withdrawal from the programme.
- Family members (especially children and elders) reporting discomfort.

Capacity discipline: The Host Sustainability Charter governs individual limits. If a
significant share of active hosts show overload signals, programme load must be reduced
programme-wide.

### 3. Beach Capacity

Describes the sustainable limit of tourism group use of beaches and coastal zones
without degrading resident access, natural environment, or sense of place.

Governing question: When does tourism presence on a public beach shift from compatible
use to occupation?

Overload signals:

- Resident displacement from beach areas during ZILWA activity windows.
- Litter, degradation, or disturbance of the coastal environment.
- Crowding at access points or focal experience locations.
- Local fishing, recreation, or cultural beach use being disrupted.

Capacity discipline: Group-size limits, timing windows, and designated-area protocols
must be set and monitored before programme launch, not in response to complaints.

### 4. Environmental Capacity

Describes the tolerance threshold of reef, coastal, and marine ecosystems for human
presence, particularly in sensitive low-tide and nearshore zones.

Governing question: Is the ecosystem recovering naturally between visits, or is
cumulative pressure causing irreversible change?

Overload signals:

- Visible damage to reef or seabed from footfall, anchoring, or handling.
- Changes in marine species behaviour or abundance attributable to tourism pressure.
- Expert or NGO warnings about habitat stress in programme areas.
- Degraded water quality near high-activity locations.

Capacity discipline: An automatic hold triggers when environmental stress indicators
exceed agreed thresholds. Hold authority belongs to the environmental steward role,
not to commercial or operational management.

### 5. Cultural Capacity

Describes the ability of traditions, practices, stories, and cultural expression to
survive tourism contact without being distorted, exhausted, or converted into performance.

Governing question: At what point does sharing culture for tourism begin to change
the culture itself?

Overload signals:

- Cultural practices simplified, abbreviated, or reframed for visitor entertainment.
- Knowledge holders or tradition carriers showing fatigue or reluctance.
- Community members reporting that sacred or private elements are being shared
  informally.
- Tourism framing beginning to replace community self-definition.

Capacity discipline: Cultural stewards (elders, knowledge holders, community leaders)
hold authority to define and enforce cultural capacity limits. ZILWA governance defers
to that authority unconditionally.

## Operational Implication

Capacity limits across all five dimensions must be:

- Defined by qualified review before programme launch.
- Monitored on a regular, documented cycle.
- Reported transparently to community stakeholders.
- Linked to automatic pause/hold triggers when thresholds are approached.
- Revisited as the programme evolves and conditions change.

Capacity is not a static number. It is a living management discipline.
