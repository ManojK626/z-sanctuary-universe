# ZILWA Child Protection Charter

> **Governance charter only. Not legal advice. Legal review required before any child-facing activity is activated.**

## Charter Purpose

This charter establishes the child-protection principles that must underpin every ZILWA
activity, concept, or programme involving children or young people. No child-facing
experience may proceed without these protections being operationally confirmed by a
qualified human reviewer.

## 1. Child-First Principle

The safety, dignity, and wellbeing of every child is the highest priority in every ZILWA
decision. No commercial, hospitality, community, or reputational interest outweighs a
child's protection.

Children are guests of trust. They are not props, performers, or participants in adult
tourism narratives.

## 2. Safeguarding Expectations

- Every person in a child-facing role must be subject to role vetting and background
  screening before any interaction with children, regardless of informality of the setting.
- Safeguarding responsibilities must be clearly assigned to named accountable roles with
  no ambiguity.
- All child-facing team members must receive safeguarding orientation before beginning
  any programme.
- Safeguarding standards must align with the most protective applicable law or
  recognised international standard.

## 3. Supervision Principles

- No child may be left in an unsupervised or ad hoc situation with any adult who is not
  their parent or verified guardian.
- The two-adult principle applies: no child should ever be alone with a single unverified
  adult in a non-public setting.
- Activity ratios (children per supervisor) must be defined for every child-facing
  experience and enforced as a hard operating limit.
- Supervision must be active and aware, not incidental.

## 4. Photography and Media Restrictions

- No photography, filming, or media capture involving a child may take place without the
  explicit prior written consent of the child's parent or legal guardian.
- Consent is specific, not blanket. Each use case requires its own documented consent.
- Children's images must never be used in marketing, social media, or promotional
  materials without specific, informed, documented guardian permission.
- Children have the right to refuse to be photographed at any time, regardless of any
  consent previously given.

## 5. Consent Principles

- Consent for any child-facing activity must be given by the child's parent or legal
  guardian in advance of participation.
- Consent must be informed: parents/guardians must understand the activity, the
  supervision arrangements, and any associated risk.
- Consent is revocable at any time without penalty or pressure.
- Children's expressed preferences and comfort must be respected within activities.

## 6. Incident Reporting Expectations

- Any safeguarding concern, however minor it appears, must be reported immediately to
  the designated safeguarding lead.
- The reporting chain must be clear, accessible, and known to all programme participants.
- No safeguarding concern may be dismissed, minimised, or managed informally.
- Serious incidents must trigger escalation to relevant authorities in accordance with
  applicable law.
- A post-incident review must occur for every safeguarding concern regardless of
  outcome.

## 7. Permanently Prohibited Activities

The following are unconditionally prohibited and may not be authorised by any ZILWA
operator, partner, or community host:

- Any unsupervised interaction between a child and an unverified adult.
- Any photography or recording of a child without documented guardian consent.
- Any health data collection beyond the legal minimum strictly required for safety.
- Any informal childcare arrangement outside a licensed, documented, supervised framework.
- Any activity that places a child in a performative, commercial, or promotional role.
- Any situation that isolates a child from their parent/guardian without full, prior,
  documented consent.

## 8. Legal Review Requirement

Before any child-facing programme, kids-club concept, family activity, or health
information handling practice is operationally activated:

- Independent legal review must be obtained in the applicable jurisdiction.
- Safeguarding compliance assessment must confirm alignment with local child-protection
  law and any relevant international standards.
- Insurance and liability review must cover the specific activity type and age group.
- This charter must be tested against regulatory expectations and updated accordingly.

## Charter Status

This charter is a governance starting point. It does not replace professional legal
advice, regulatory compliance, or licensed safeguarding frameworks. Implementation
requires independent human review and sign-off before any child-facing activity proceeds.
