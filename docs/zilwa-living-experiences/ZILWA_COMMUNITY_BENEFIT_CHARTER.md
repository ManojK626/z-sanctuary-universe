# ZILWA Community Benefit Charter

> **Governance charter only. Community benefit arrangements require legal and
> community governance review before formalisation.**

## Charter Purpose

ZILWA exists within and because of its community. This charter defines the
principles that ensure ZILWA operates as a genuine partner to the Flic en Flac
and wider Mauritius community — not as an extractive tourism overlay.

## 1. Community-First Principle

- Community wellbeing precedes programme growth.
- No expansion, partnership, or operational change proceeds if it demonstrably
  harms the community.
- Community consent is ongoing, not a one-time approval. It must be renewed and
  reaffirmed as the programme evolves.
- Community members are partners in shaping ZILWA, not just suppliers of experience.

## 2. Local Benefit Expectations

- A meaningful proportion of the value generated by ZILWA must remain within the
  local community through direct payments, employment, and supply chain choices.
- Local micro-enterprises, artisans, food producers, and transport operators must
  receive fair access to partnership opportunities.
- Benefit distribution must be actively monitored and publicly reported.
  Concentration of value in a small number of participants is a governance warning
  sign requiring investigation and correction.
- Long-term local employment and skill development should be a stated objective, not
  an incidental by-product.

## 3. Public-Space Respect

- Public beaches, parks, village paths, and communal spaces belong to the whole
  community.
- ZILWA groups must not monopolise, degrade, or restrict public-space access for
  residents.
- Group-size limits, timing windows, and route guidance must protect resident use
  of shared spaces.
- Any damage to public spaces attributable to ZILWA activities must be remediated
  promptly.

## 4. Transparency Expectations

- ZILWA must publish clear information about how value is distributed, who benefits,
  and what community obligations are being honoured.
- Transparency reporting should be accessible to community members in plain language.
- Key programme decisions that affect community life must be made with community
  input and communicated openly.
- Community members must be able to verify benefit claims, not simply accept
  them as stated.

## 5. Grievance Pathway Concept

- Any community member who believes ZILWA has caused harm, acted unfairly, or
  failed its community obligations must have a clear, accessible, and safe way to
  raise their concern.
- Grievances must be acknowledged promptly, investigated fairly, and resolved
  transparently.
- The grievance pathway must be independent of ZILWA operational management to
  ensure impartiality.
- Resolution outcomes must be communicated back to the person who raised the
  grievance.
- No retaliation of any kind toward a person who raises a community grievance
  is acceptable.

## 6. Anti-Exploitation Principles

- Cultural practices, traditions, stories, and community spaces are not commercial
  raw materials.
- ZILWA must never package community identity in a way that reduces it to an
  entertainment product for external consumption.
- Vulnerable community members — elders, children, people in economic difficulty —
  must never be placed in positions that compromise their dignity or agency for
  programme benefit.
- Any partner, operator, or programme element that exploits community vulnerability
  must be removed immediately.
- Commercial success does not justify community harm.

## Charter Status

This charter defines governance intent. Community benefit structures, grievance
mechanisms, and benefit-sharing arrangements require legal, financial, and community
governance expertise to formalise properly. This charter is the starting obligation,
not the finished architecture.
