# ZILWA Community Impact Assessment

## Purpose

Assess likely effects on community life, households, local businesses, public spaces, and social fabric in and around Flic en Flac under a hospitality-led experience model.

## Community Benefit Potential

- Household income diversification through hosted experiences and local service participation.
- Increased demand for local crafts, food, guides, and transport micro-enterprises.
- Stronger storytelling value around place, memory, and elder-guided heritage.
- Potential reduction of tourism value leakage when local participation is fairly structured.

## Community Risk Exposure

### Public-space pressure

- Beaches and village routes may face crowding, litter pressure, and privacy erosion.
- Peak-hour activity could displace regular resident use of shared spaces.

### Household and social pressure

- Host families may absorb emotional and domestic labor beyond sustainable limits.
- Inter-household tension may grow if participation and income distribution are perceived as unfair.

### Cultural and identity pressure

- Traditions may be simplified for visitor consumption.
- Community memory could shift from lived practice to staged performance.

## Stakeholder Impact Map

| Stakeholder | Potential Benefit | Potential Harm | Required Protection |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Host families | New income channels and social recognition | Burnout, privacy loss, role strain | Shift caps, rest doctrine, opt-out rights |
| Elders/knowledge holders | Recognition and compensation opportunity | Exploitation, ownership ambiguity | Consent, rights charter, compensation protocol |
| Children and youth | Cultural pride and exposure to opportunities | Safety risks, performative pressure | Safeguarding doctrine, guardian control |
| Local businesses | Visitor demand growth | Uneven access to participation | Transparent partner selection and rotation |
| General residents | Infrastructure improvements possible | Congestion, noise, exclusion | Carrying-capacity and grievance mechanisms |

## Equity and Benefit-Sharing Governance Needs

1. Transparent benefit ledger tracking value distribution across community groups.
2. Rotational inclusion model to avoid concentration in a small operator circle.
3. Minimum local-retention threshold for gross program value.
4. Independent community review panel for disputes and fairness checks.

## Community Protections Missing in Current Branch Evidence

- No visible ZILWA-specific doctrine files in this branch for consent, grievance, or burden thresholds.
- No published local carrying-capacity model for beach/village impact.
- No host-family sustainability matrix or overload triggers.
- No formal “community veto” mechanism for sensitive activities.

## Recommended Community Safeguards Before Any Expansion

1. Community consent charter with annual renewal and withdrawal pathways.
2. Public grievance channel with response SLAs and independent escalation.
3. Burden threshold controls (daily group caps, route zoning, quiet-hour commitments).
4. Household protection standards (maximum hosting load, privacy boundaries, cooldown windows).
5. Community outcome dashboard: jobs, income distribution, complaints, incident trends, ecological pressure.

## Assessment Outcome

Community upside exists, but only if governance assures fairness, consent, protection, and reversible scaling. Without these, social trust risks becoming the primary failure mode.

## Community Impact Verdict

**AMBER** — acceptable only as governance-preparation phase; operational rollout should remain on HOLD until community safeguards are codified and independently reviewed.
