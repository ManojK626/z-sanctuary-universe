# ZILWA Accessibility Charter

> **Governance charter only. Accessibility design requires qualified specialist
> review before implementation.**

## Charter Purpose

ZILWA commits to making its experiences genuinely inclusive and dignified for every
traveller, regardless of physical ability, sensory capacity, age, or cognitive profile.
Inclusion is a design responsibility, not an afterthought.

## 1. Mobility Considerations

- Experience routes and venues must be assessed for mobility accessibility before
  guests are invited to participate.
- Accessible alternatives must exist for every activity that has a physical mobility
  requirement. No guest should be excluded from the essence of an experience due
  to a mobility limitation.
- Seating, rest points, shade, and transfer aids must be available along all
  experience routes at appropriate intervals.
- Transport arrangements must include options suitable for wheelchair users,
  walking-frame users, and guests with limited endurance.
- Physical access details (surfaces, gradients, steps, distances) must be published
  honestly so guests can make informed choices.

## 2. Hearing Accessibility

- Key orientation, safety, and activity information must be available in visual
  formats alongside spoken formats.
- Captioned or text-based alternatives must be available for guided experiences.
- Hosts and ambassadors must receive basic awareness training for communicating
  clearly with guests who have hearing differences.
- Interpreter or communication-support access must be available upon request.

## 3. Visual Accessibility

- Tactile orientation aids must be available at key decision points along
  experience routes.
- High-contrast wayfinding must be considered in any printed or physical materials.
- Audio-first guidance options must be available for guests with visual impairments.
- Experience briefings must be available in accessible formats before arrival so
  guests can prepare.

## 4. Neurodiversity Awareness

- Sensory-safe scheduling options must be considered: quieter, lower-stimulus
  experience windows that avoid peak crowd times, loud environments, or sudden
  changes.
- Clear, predictable activity structures with advance briefings help neurodiverse
  guests feel safe and prepared.
- Hosts and ambassadors must receive awareness training on neurodiversity and
  respectful communication.
- Guests must never be asked to justify or explain their needs in detail.

## 5. Senior Traveller Support

- Pacing flexibility must be built into all experiences: seniors must never feel
  rushed or left behind.
- Heat, hydration, and sun exposure management must be actively considered in
  experience design, particularly for outdoor and beach contexts.
- Medication timing and rest needs must be accommodated without drawing attention.
- Physical and cognitive fatigue must be recognised as natural and accommodated
  without judgment.

## 6. Communication Accessibility

- Experience communication must avoid jargon, cultural assumptions, and ambiguous
  phrasing.
- Written materials must be available in plain language.
- Multilingual support must be considered given Mauritius's linguistic diversity.
- Communication channels must include non-verbal options wherever possible.

## 7. Dignity-Centred Design

- Accessibility adjustments must be offered naturally and discreetly, never in
  a manner that singles out or embarrasses a guest.
- Guests define their own needs. Assumptions about what someone requires must
  never be made based on appearance or perceived condition.
- Independence and autonomy must be supported, not substituted. Assistance is
  available but not imposed.
- Privacy of any disclosed access need must be respected absolutely.
- Every guest deserves the same quality of experience. Accessible options must be
  comparable in value and richness to standard options.

## Charter Status

This charter defines the aspiration and governance direction. Realising it requires
input from qualified accessibility specialists, disabled travellers, and relevant
advocacy organisations before any experience design is finalised.
