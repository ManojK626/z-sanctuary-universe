# ZILWA Host Sustainability Charter

> **Governance charter only. Host participation arrangements require legal review
> for applicable labour, compensation, and liability obligations.**

## Charter Purpose

ZILWA's ability to deliver meaningful, community-led experiences depends entirely on
the sustained wellbeing of host families and community participants. This charter
defines the non-negotiable protections that preserve the humanity and sustainability
of every host.

## 1. Host Wellbeing

- Host wellbeing is a primary responsibility of the ZILWA operating model, not a
  secondary consideration.
- Emotional labour, domestic disruption, and social fatigue are real costs that must
  be acknowledged and mitigated, not ignored.
- Hosts are not service infrastructure. They are people whose generosity enables
  the programme.
- Regular wellbeing check-ins must be built into the operating rhythm, not left
  to individual self-reporting.

## 2. Participation Limits

- Maximum hosting frequency must be defined before any programme launches.
- Limits cover: consecutive days hosting, guests per visit, visits per week, visits
  per month, and cumulative seasonal load.
- Hosts must be informed of their limit schedule in advance and it must not be
  changed without their agreement.
- Operational demand does not override hosting limits.

## 3. Rest Periods

- Mandatory rest periods between hosting engagements must be built into scheduling
  as structural rules, not soft guidelines.
- Minimum rest periods must be defined and documented before programme launch.
- No host may be scheduled in a way that voids their rest entitlement, regardless
  of external demand or operational pressure.

## 4. Right to Decline

- Every host retains the right to decline any individual guest, group, visit, or
  activity type at any time, without requiring justification.
- Decline decisions are private and may not be shared, logged in a manner that
  disadvantages the host, or used in host performance assessments.
- Hosts may temporarily or permanently withdraw from the programme at any time.
- Declining or withdrawing does not affect a host's standing in the community or
  their eligibility for other community benefits.

## 5. No-Retaliation Principle

- No host who exercises their right to decline, rest, modify their participation,
  or raise a concern will face retaliation of any kind.
- Retaliation includes: reduced referrals, exclusion from benefits, social pressure,
  negative representation, or programme disadvantage.
- Retaliation claims must have an independent resolution pathway that protects the
  host from further harm during investigation.

## 6. Fair-Value Principle

- Hosts must receive fair compensation for their time, resources, and hospitality.
- Compensation must be transparent, predictable, and paid promptly.
- Value distribution must not concentrate disproportionately in intermediary or
  commercial layers at the expense of host families.
- Hosts must be informed of the full value chain: what guests pay, what ZILWA
  retains, and what reaches the host.
- Market-rate benchmarks must inform compensation design.

## 7. Family-First Principle

- The needs of the host's household and family take precedence over tourism
  scheduling.
- Hosts must never feel pressured to prioritise guest experiences over family
  occasions, health needs, household rhythms, or personal circumstances.
- Family comfort and privacy within shared domestic spaces must be protected at
  all times during hosting.
- Children within host households are not programme participants and carry the
  full protections of the Child Protection Charter.

## Charter Status

This charter defines intent and governance expectations. It does not constitute a
legal employment or service contract. All host participation arrangements require
legal review for applicable labour, compensation, liability, and safety obligations
in the Mauritius jurisdiction before formalisation.
