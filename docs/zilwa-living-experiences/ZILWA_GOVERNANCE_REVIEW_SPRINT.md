## 1) Hospitality Review

### Strength signals

- Strong emotional proposition: community-led, memory-rich, place-connected hospitality.
- Potential to differentiate from transactional resort models.
- Could create higher guest loyalty if authenticity and safety are both proven.

### Operational pressure points

- Host-family and ambassador capacity likely to fail first (fatigue, uneven service standards, emotional labor overload).
- Experience quality may become inconsistent without strict playbooks and limits per day/week.
- Cross-cultural expectation mismatches can create guest dissatisfaction and resident discomfort.

### What would fail first

1. Staffing rhythm and host sustainability.
2. Daily quality consistency across family/community touchpoints.
3. Incident escalation readiness (medical, transport, safeguarding, weather).

## 2) Community Impact Review

### Likely benefits

- Income diversification for local households and small vendors.
- Cultural pride and intergenerational storytelling opportunities.
- More distributed tourism value outside traditional resort concentration.

### Likely unintended consequences

- Community space pressure (public beaches, village routes, noise, congestion).
- Inflation and rent pressure if tourism demand concentrates in family zones.
- Identity dilution if tradition is repackaged to satisfy short-stay entertainment patterns.

### Missing protections

- Community carrying-capacity thresholds.
- Benefit-sharing transparency (who earns/how much/how often).
- Resident grievance and remediation pathway with independent oversight.

## 3) Elder Knowledge Review

### Core risk

Without explicit governance, elder knowledge can be extracted, branded, and monetized without durable ownership rights.

### Required stewardship upgrades

- Prior informed consent framework for every cultural element used.
- Attribution and compensation policy for knowledge holders.
- Community veto right on recordings, storytelling formats, and commercialization channels.
- “Sacred/private/not-for-tourism” knowledge boundary register.

## 4) Child Safety Review

### Regulatory expectations likely to apply

- Mandatory background checks and role screening for all child-facing roles.
- Two-adult rule for child interactions.
- Parent/guardian consent architecture for each activity.
- Mandatory incident logging and immediate escalation protocol.
- Age-banded activity design and transport safeguards.

### Must remain permanently prohibited

- Any unsupervised child-tourist interaction with unvetted adults.
- Any health-document collection beyond minimum legal requirement.
- Any photography or media capture of children without explicit guardian consent.
- Any “informal childcare” outside licensed/supervised doctrine.

## 5) Environmental Review

### Ecological risk pattern

- Reef restoration and low-tide experiences can unintentionally increase ecological pressure through footfall and anchor-like behavior in sensitive areas.
- “Nature intimacy” programming can become extraction-by-visitation if not capped.

### Required controls before expansion

- Site-specific ecological carrying-capacity rules.
- No-go zones and seasonal closures.
- Certified guide-only access to sensitive marine areas.
- Continuous environmental monitoring with stop-trigger thresholds.
- “Pause authority” when habitat stress indicators rise.

## 6) Accessibility Review

### Inclusion baseline status

Concept appears potentially inclusive in intent, but no evidence of accessible-by-design operating doctrine is visible in this branch.

### Recommended adjustments

- Mobility: route grading, transfer aids, accessible transport options, fallback indoor alternatives.
- Hearing: visual cue systems, captioned orientation materials, interpreter protocol for key experiences.
- Vision: tactile orientation, high-contrast wayfinding, audio-first guidance options.
- Neurodiversity: sensory-safe options, low-stimulus scheduling windows, clear expectation briefs.
- Seniors: pacing options, rest-point density, hydration/heat doctrine, medication-aware planning.

## 7) Legal and Regulatory Review

### Highest legal review areas

- Home dining/household-hosted experiences (food safety, licensing, liability).
- Tourism transport arrangements (duty-of-care, insurance, driver compliance).
- Volunteer and community participation structures (labor classification, waivers, safeguarding obligations).
- Health-related doctrines/passports (privacy, data minimization, retention boundaries).
- Marine/coastal activity permissions and protected-area compliance.

### Highest compliance burden concepts

1. Child/family programs.
2. Household dining and in-home hosting.
3. Transport-linked guest movement.
4. Reef and shoreline activities.
5. Any health-status handling process.

## 8) Investor Review

### Differentiation potential

- Real community integration and elder knowledge stewardship can be a defensible narrative if proven ethically and operationally.

### Likely investor challenges

- “Show me repeatable quality without cultural commodification.”
- “Show me margin profile under strict safety and compliance controls.”
- “Show me incident governance, insurance posture, and legal exposure controls.”
- “Show me expansion discipline and stop-go gates.”

### Evidence investors will request

- Pilot outcomes with independently validated safety/community metrics.
- Host retention and burnout indicators.
- Community benefit distribution data.
- Incident rates, response times, and post-incident corrective actions.
- Regulatory counsel opinion set by activity type.

## 9) Red Team Review

### Critic attack lines likely to emerge

- “Culture extraction packaged as tourism.”
- “Community burden shifted to vulnerable households.”
- “Child/family safety claims without auditable controls.”
- “Environmental branding without hard carrying-capacity rules.”

### Contradictions to resolve early

- Authentic spontaneity vs standardized safety controls.
- Community intimacy vs privacy boundaries.
- Local ownership narrative vs investor scalability pressure.

## Recommended Doctrine Changes

1. Add a **Community Benefit and Burden Ledger** with monthly public reporting.
2. Add a **Child Safeguarding Non-Negotiables Doctrine** (hard prohibitions + escalation chain).
3. Add an **Elder Knowledge Rights Charter** (consent, attribution, compensation, veto rights).
4. Add an **Environmental Carrying-Capacity Protocol** with automatic hold triggers.
5. Add an **Accessibility-by-Design Standard** with minimum acceptance criteria per activity.
6. Add a **Legal Readiness Gate** requiring sign-off before each activity class is activated.
7. Add a **Host Sustainability Standard** (shift caps, rest cycles, mental load monitoring).

## Recommended HOLD Items (Do Not Advance)

- Kids-club or family-care experiences without licensed safeguarding framework.
- Home dining experiences before food-safety and liability architecture is approved.
- Reef/low-tide expansion before ecological threshold controls are live.
- Health-passport-like doctrines without legal privacy minimization sign-off.
- Any scale-up narrative before pilot evidence proves community-positive outcomes.

## Overall Verdict

Verdict: AMBER (Hold-Conditional)

Proceed only as governance hardening. Do not launch operational tourism features until doctrine artifacts, safeguards, and legal/community controls are formalized and independently reviewed.
